The Model Is Not the Hard Part · 2nd MedAI Summit · 28 Oct 2026

Four questions for any medical AI tool

A one-page checklist for medical and pharmacy students · Ali Ganjizadeh, MD, MBA

An AI tool performs like a diagnostic test and lives like a drug, so judge it like both. None of these questions is about the model. All of them draw on clinical training.

Download the one-page PDF
1

Data provenance

Where did the data come from, and do those patients look like mine?

  • Where did the data and labels come from? Do those patients, sites and devices look like mine?Like a drug’s trial population
  • How were the “right answers” decided, and was it tested at other hospitals and in subgroups?Like a gold standard, then replication

From the talk: purple surgical ink on benign moles cut a melanoma AI’s specificity from 84% to 46% (Winkler, JAMA Dermatol 2019).

2

Label definition

What exactly does it predict, and who acts on it, when?

  • Does it predict what matters, or a stand-in such as cost or a billing code?Like a surrogate endpoint
  • Who acts on the output, for which patient, at what moment? Does it arrive before we’d know anyway?Like a drug’s indication

From the talk: an algorithm that predicted healthcare cost instead of need gave Black patients the same risk score as white patients who were less sick (Obermeyer, Science 2019).

3

Evaluation

What evidence is there beyond accuracy, at my prevalence?

  • What is the positive predictive value (PPV) at my prevalence, and how many false alarms come with each true case?Like a screening test’s PPV
  • How far up the ladder has it climbed: accuracy on past data, silent mode, a supervised pilot, a randomised trial of patient outcomes?Like trial phases, preclinical to Phase III

From the talk: at 1% prevalence, a tool that is 95% sensitive and 95% specific has a PPV of about 16%. Five of every six alerts are false.

4

After go-live

Who watches it once it’s in use?

  • Is there a label stating its intended use, population, and when not to use it?Like a package insert
  • Who monitors it after go-live, and what happens when the scanner, workflow or patient mix changes?Like pharmacovigilance

From the talk: Duke’s “Model Facts” label for its sepsis model states who it is for and warns, for example, that it is not for ICU patients (Sendak, npj Digit Med 2020).

Red flags: no clear answer on where the training data came from · only accuracy or AUC reported, with no PPV at your prevalence · never tested at a hospital like yours · nobody named to monitor it after go-live.
